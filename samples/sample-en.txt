The water cycle describes how water moves continuously between the Earth and the atmosphere. It has four main stages: evaporation, condensation, precipitation, and collection.

Evaporation happens when heat from the Sun turns liquid water from oceans, lakes, and rivers into water vapour, which rises into the air. Plants also release water vapour through their leaves in a process called transpiration.

As the vapour rises and cools, it condenses into tiny water droplets around dust particles, forming clouds. This stage is called condensation.

When the droplets grow heavy enough, they fall back to Earth as precipitation — rain, snow, sleet, or hail. Some of this water collects in rivers, lakes, and oceans (collection), while some soaks into the ground and becomes groundwater.

The Sun is the main source of energy that drives the whole cycle, and gravity pulls the water back down. Without the water cycle, fresh water would not be renewed, and life on land would not survive.
